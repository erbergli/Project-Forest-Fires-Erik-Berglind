# Monocultures and the spread of forest fires

**Erik Berglind - Complex Adaptive Systems**

## Introduction and approach

The frequency of forest fires is increasing worldwide [1], which could lead to catastrophic effects on the environment nad our civilization as a whole. Research points towards climate change, longer periods of droughts, and the way forests are managed and planted as a few examples of contributors to the increase and severity of forest fires. Large monocultural forests of have been shown be particularly vulnurable to the spread of forest fires. Suprisingly, acts of arguable benevolence such as replanting lost areas of forest could potentially add to the problem if the methods of planting promote a monoculture [2] Understanding how methods of planting and farming trees affect forest fire size and severity is therefore higly relevant in order to better adapt to an even warmer and drier future. This project aims to study how quickly forest fires spread in both homogenous and hetrogenous ecosystems. Furthermore it aims to understand how common ways of planting forests could give rise to more severe fires.

To investigate the problem, a fire will be simulated over several different forest configurations, such as a randomly grown forest or a planned and planted forest. The velocity of the fire spreading will be evaluated for each type of forest to determine the effect of the previously mentioned parameters.

To ensure consistency, parameters such as rainfall, humidity, or terrain will not be considered in this encapsulated project, meaning that all forests will be grown on the same terrain. All of these parameters would be interesting to investigate, but do not fit in the scope of the project.
